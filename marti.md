
In molecular biology, a **target gene** is a specific gene that a protein, often a transcription factor or RNA-binding protein, interacts with to regulate its expression. This interaction can either activate or repress the target gene, leading to changes in the amount of the protein encoded by that gene. 

https://tp53.cancer.gov/target_genes

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28288132/

Which target genes of p53 are important? 

- The transcription factor p53 itself is solely an activator of transcription.

- Gene downregulation by p53 is indirect and requires p21.

- Functional p53 binding sites are independent of cell type and treatment.

- Most functional p53 binding sites are found in proximal promoters.

- Functional p53 binding sites consist of two decameric half-sites, and likely do not contain spacers in between.

- Noncanonical binding sites, including half-sites, appear to be non-functional.

- P53 mostly acts alone to activate target genes, and does not depend on cofactors binding to the same promoter.

- The number of true p53 target genes is limited, and likely does not exceed a few hundred.
